# EUP Prose Voice Standard v1 (2026-08-12)

**Authority:** CEO-issued. Governs every customer-facing fixed sentence in every EUP product — spine blocks, composer templates, clause libraries, table cells, asks. Applied product by product through each Conversion's prose pass; every change ratified by the CEO before it is byte-pinned. Prompts invoke it as "the EUP Prose Voice Standard."

## The voice in one paragraph
The document is counsel writing *for* a client who is working toward compliance — cooperative, constructive, and plain. The company is the actor: it identifies, states, describes, believes, provides. The law is the framework: where a statute or rule imposes conditions, the document says what the law requires, enumerates the conditions, and cites the provision. The assessment is the judge: its determinations are earned from the record and stated plainly, favorable or not. No bureaucratic filler, no machine vocabulary, no hedging tics, no drama.

## Rules

**1. Cooperative company-as-actor framing.** The company works toward a goal; the document records its progress. Write "The company has identified the following facts as mitigating factors, as Article XYZ requires" — not "Company shares the following record for Article XYZ in the intake." Active, present-tense verbs of the identifies/states/describes/believes family. Never "the record + verb" ("the record shows/reflects/indicates/establishes"); never "the intake," "the submission," "the form," or "the questionnaire" in customer prose.

**2. The law's conditions are spelled out, with the citation.** Where a provision imposes a requirement the document is testing, name what the provision requires and cite it: "Article 28(3) requires a written contract covering the processing's subject matter, duration, nature and purpose, the data and the data subjects, and the controller's instructions (Art. 28(3) GDPR)." Enumerations of statutory conditions come only from approved corpus rows via the registry — never paraphrased from memory. Citation style per the CEO ruling: spelled out in running prose ("Article 35(1)"), abbreviated in parentheticals ("(Art. 35(1))"), registry form in citation fields and Tables of Authorities.

**3. Plain, grammatically flowing, constructive.** Short declarative sentences over stacked clauses. No label-colon data dumps inside prose ("Cause: X. Pathway: Y."). No tautologies ("severe in severity"). No "(s)" pluralization hacks — number words one through nine, digits above. Dates in human form ("1 May 2026"). Customer verbatim enters through quotation with a colon or "in its own words," via the shared splice-normalizer.

**4. Constructive honesty — credit first, verdicts earned.** Cooperative tone never manufactures a conclusion. Where the record supports a determination, state it plainly and cite the basis. Where it does not, say what the company HAS provided first, then name the specific facts that would complete the point ("The company describes its retention rule; recording the retention period for the diagnosis category would complete the Article 5(1)(e) analysis (Art. 5(1)(e) GDPR)"). Absences are noted rather than filled; negatives are never asserted from silence — the company's answer is the basis, not the assessment's finding. Only verdict-blocking gaps become asks; everything else is a credit-first residual.

**5. No machine or internal vocabulary, ever.** No node names, field keys, test ids, pipeline terms ("risk_register node," "ledger order," "TEST-STATES," normalizer tokens), no drafting-process language ("to be finalised"), no grader or schema vocabulary. Internal mechanics live in code comments and telemetry, not in the customer's document.

**6. Counsel-referral and ownership discipline (unchanged).** Actions belong to internal roles the company names; counsel-delegation language lives only in the fixed disclaimer zones.

**7. Repetition across typed rows is acceptable; repetition of caveats is not.** A ratified per-row template may repeat its structure across a register — that is legal drafting. But a caveat ("proposed until re-scored") states once per document, with later rows referencing it ("on the same proposed basis").

## Drafting process (per product)
Lovable drafts proposed prose with a two-lens ensemble instruction — a senior privacy lawyer for accuracy of every statutory statement, and a plain-English editor for flow and economy — then presents COMPLETE current-vs-proposed text generated from the shipped bytes (never a stored copy). The CEO reviews, edits, ratifies; approved bytes land pinned with a version bump; pinpoint byte-checks re-verified. Statutory-quotation sentences keep their corpus-verified wording exactly.

## Worked example (the CEO's, generalized)
Weak: "Company shares the following record for Article 32 in the intake."
Standard: "Article 32 requires technical and organisational measures appropriate to the risk, including — as appropriate — pseudonymisation, encryption, and the ability to restore availability after an incident (Art. 32(1) GDPR). The company has identified the following measures toward that requirement:"
Then the table or verbatim follows; the assessment's determination of sufficiency, where one is reached, comes after — earned, cited, and stated plainly.

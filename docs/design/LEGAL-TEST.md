# THE LEGAL TEST (CEO-adopted 2026-07-26 — standing methodology for all AI legal-document products)

Before wiring any AI legal-document product (and at every cited-law change), classify EVERY conclusion the document asserts:

Q1 — Outcome commanded by a bright-line rule applied to ascertainable facts? → TYPE R (Rule). Fully deterministic: Pass-1 derives; polarity-determinism validation applies; model disagreement is a defect; report states conclusion plainly with pinpoint. Examples: 11 CCR § 7150(b) triggers; § 7121(a) cohorts; Civ. Code § 1798.140(d)(1) applicability; state breach-notification counts.

Q2 — Law prescribes factors but leaves outcome to weighing? → TYPE W (Weighing). Pass-1 assembles a FACTOR TABLE (every statutorily required factor, each anchored to ledger fact or registry text; completeness hard-validated; conclusion NOT polarity-validated). Model proposes conclusion with reasoning citing only validated factors (consistency-validated). CALIBRATED RENDERING: firm language only on lopsided factor tables; close balances MUST be disclosed as close with what-would-tip-it stated; flat certainty on a close balance = post-render hard reject. Grading: factor completeness, anchoring, reasoning consistency, calibrated tone — NEVER a single expected outcome. Deterministic expected-answer checks are valid ONLY for Type R. Examples: § 7152(a)(5) benefit/harm weighing; Art 6(1)(f) balancing; Art 35(7)(b) necessity/proportionality; Art 33 risk-to-rights.

Q3 — Conclusion the document should not reach? → TYPE J (Reserved Judgment). Present the completed analysis; pose the decision in customer-question/counsel-voice form. A product boundary, not a capability gap. Examples: ultimate validity verdicts on close balances; anything functioning as contested legal advice.

INFRASTRUCTURE: per product — conclusion inventory _shared/legal-test/<product>-conclusions.ts (every assertable conclusion tagged R/W/J with statutory anchor, versioned) + factor registries _shared/factors/<product>-factors.ts (statutory factor lists for each Type W test, corpus-quoted, pin-tested like verified-authority registries).

FIRST-PASS INVENTORIES (from the 2026-07-26 joint review; refine at each product's build turn): cppa-risk — R: 7150(b)/7121(a)/1798.140(d)(1)/7157; W: 7152(a)(5) weighing (core), severity/likelihood; J: proceed-call on close balance. lia — R: lawfulness edges; W: necessity+balancing (core), reasonable expectations; J: validity verdict on close balance. dpia — R: Art 35(1)/(3) triggers, Art 36 duty; W: 35(7)(b) necessity/proportionality, residual risk; J: risk-acceptability ultimate call. ir — R: state count thresholds, deadlines; W: Art 33 risk-to-rights, Art 34 high-risk. cyber — R: 7120/7121/7123; W: 7122 adequacy. governance — R: presence/absence; W: adequacy characterizations. dpa — R: 28(3)(a)-(h) completeness. admt — R: 7200-7222 obligations, 7001(ddd) scope; W: human-involvement adequacy. registration/biometric — nearly all R.

Q4 — CORPUS ENGAGEMENT (CEO-adopted 2026-07-26; standing law, applies to every Type W test AND glossed Type R edges). The corpus is a GUIDE wherever a test or balancing of interests is required, and its engagement is a mandatory role in both passes (and, when engaged, a dedicated additional pass — see Pass G in TWO-PASS-ARCHITECTURE.md §2.6). Five requirements: (a) GUIDANCE MAPPING AUTHORED AT REGISTRY TIME — every factor-registry row (_shared/factors/<product>-factors.ts) carries guidance_refs[] binding interpretive corpus sources deterministically: FSOR commentary (cppa_fsor_commentary and cppa_fsor_callouts) for CPPA tests; EDPB guideline rows for GDPR tests; corpus-quoted, pin-tested, status=final only; runtime lookup is deterministic (no semantic search at render time for authored bindings — semantic layer is candidate-set construction only, resolved to explicit refs before rendering). (b) ANALOGY LAYER GOVERNED — enforcement_actions usage obeys the CEO-adopted eligibility bar: QUOTE-SAFE tier permits verbatim quotes with pinpoint; ROW-GROUNDED tier permits characterizations grounded in the row; INELIGIBLE tier never surfaces (neither quoted nor characterized nor cited). Analogy entries carry the tier explicitly; a render referencing an ineligible row is a hard reject. (c) GAPS ARE INGESTION WORK, NOT IMPROVISATION — a test with no corpus guidance renders on statutory factors alone WITH EXPRESS DISCLOSURE ("guidance on this balance is limited"); the model MUST NOT introduce authority not present in the pre-indexed candidate set. Every empty-by-finding event auto-emits a telemetry record that feeds the T5 ingestion backlog as a ranked work item (rank = product weight × Type W frequency × recency of the empty finding). T5 sequencing, when the program resumes, is standing-input-fed by this queue. (d) FRESHNESS — superseded guidance is excluded at query time (status filter); when T5 lands a new batch in a referenced guidance family (FSOR package or EDPB guideline series), all bindings referencing that family are re-validated (pin re-run, quote re-check, supersession scan). Failed re-validation demotes the binding until re-authored. (e) AUTHORITY-DOMAIN MATCHING (CEO-ordered 2026-07-26; v2.1) — every test/analysis-unit a product runs carries a JURISDICTION TAG; all corpus candidates for that unit (statutory anchors, guidance_refs, Pass-G candidate sets, analogies) are FILTERED to the matching domain. Cross-domain authority appearing in customer-facing analysis is a HARD REJECT at BOTH authoring review AND Pass-G candidate-set construction. Domain definitions: **CPPA/CA domain** = `cppa_authorities`, `provision_texts` CA/ccpa rows, `cppa_fsor_commentary` / `cppa_fsor_callouts`, `cppa_deadlines`, California statutes (Civ. Code) (CPPA-domain corpus is BINDING-tier CA interpretive material; the FSOR's own discussion of non-CA material may be surfaced ONLY at PERSUASIVE tier per v2.2 authority-weight tiering below, never as authority). **GDPR domain** = `gdpr_articles` (EU + UK rows per sub-jurisdiction tag), `edpb_guidelines`, `provision_texts` EU/UK rows, GDPR-tagged `enforcement_actions` (eligibility bar applies). **US-state domains** (breach / biometric) = the respective state statutes when in corpus. **HYBRID PRODUCTS** (ir-playbook, governance, registration, biometric): scoping is PER ANALYSIS UNIT — a GDPR section uses GDPR corpus only, a California section uses CA corpus only, never blended within a unit; the product's conclusion inventory tags each unit with its jurisdiction. **FUTURE-PROOFING** — `enforcement_actions.jurisdiction` tags mean that future CPPA enforcement rows (once they exist and pass the eligibility bar) automatically flow into CPPA candidate sets with no architecture change. **COMPARATIVE CROSS-DOMAIN COMMENTARY** ("GDPR practice suggests…", "California has taken a similar view…") is BANNED from customer-facing output; any future comparative feature is a separate CEO decision, not a runtime capability.

Q4(e) v2.2 — AUTHORITY-WEIGHT TIERING (CEO-CORRECTED 2026-07-26; supersedes v2.1 phrasing that treated FSOR-discussed non-CA analogies as CPPA authority; that phrasing is WRONG and formally superseded). Every corpus reference in every product carries `authority_weight ∈ {binding, persuasive}`. Rules: (i) Type R conclusions may anchor ONLY on binding-tier, in-domain sources. (ii) Type W factor anchors (`factor_table` rows and their `guidance_refs[]`) are binding-tier only. (iii) `weighing_frame[]` entries may be persuasive-tier only where domain rules permit — for CPPA products, FSOR-mediated non-CA material may enter the weighing_frame at persuasive tier only, expressly rendered as such, and each such entry MUST carry `fsor_mediation_ref` pointing to the CPPA-domain FSOR row that discusses the non-CA material; GDPR/UK products carry NO persuasive bridge from U.S./California authority in ANY role (not binding, not persuasive, not analogy) — the bridge is ONE-WAY. (iv) RENDERING DISCIPLINE — persuasive material is linguistically marked in output ("by way of analogy…", "persuasive but not binding…"); marker is template-enforced and asserted post-render; a persuasive item may inform weighing color but can NEVER be the sole support for any conclusion. (v) VALIDATORS — `authority_weight` and `jurisdiction_tag` are checked on every citation binding, factor guidance_ref, and weighing_frame entry; binding-tier assertion on Type R proposition anchors and on Type W factor anchors is a HARD REJECT when violated; persuasive weighing_frame entries without `fsor_mediation_ref` are HARD REJECT; any GDPR-domain plan containing a U.S./CA-sourced entry (any tier) is HARD REJECT.

Q4(e) v2.3 — FEDERAL-QUALIFICATION / GENERALIZED FORUM RULE (CEO-CORRECTED 2026-07-26; QUALIFIES v2.2). The v2.2 sentence "Non-California law can never be binding authority for a California product" is SUPERSEDED by the CEO's corrected sentence quoted verbatim: **"Non-California law can never be binding authority for a California product UNLESS it is U.S. Federal law, including U.S. agency rulings."** Rationale: federal law applies of its own force within California under the Supremacy Clause; FTC rulings and other U.S. federal agency rulings are U.S. law applicable in California and every other U.S. state. GENERALIZED FORUM RULE (applies to any U.S.-state analysis unit — California/CPPA, Illinois/BIPA, Texas, etc.): BINDING tier = the forum state's own law + U.S. FEDERAL law (statutes, regulations, FTC and other federal agency rulings; corpus tag `jurisdiction_tag: "us-federal"`). SISTER-STATE law (another U.S. state's statutes/rulings) = PERSUASIVE/ANALOGY tier only, expressly marked. FOREIGN law follows the existing per-domain rules (CPPA: FSOR-mediated persuasive only per v2.2). GDPR/UK products UNCHANGED — no U.S. material (state OR federal) in any role; the bridge is ONE-WAY. Corpus implication: `enforcement_actions` and future corpus rows tagged `jurisdiction=us-federal` are BINDING-tier-eligible for U.S. products (the CEO-adopted eligibility bar still applies — quote-safe / row-grounded tiers govern how the row surfaces).
